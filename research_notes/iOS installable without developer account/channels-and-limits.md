# iOS install channels without a paid Apple Developer Program membership (as of 2026-11-16)

Research date: 2026-11-16. All "current" claims were checked against sources published or updated in 2025–2026; Apple's own help/support pages were fetched directly on the research date. The scenario this feeds: one personal iPhone (Czech Republic, EU, current iOS — iOS 26.x in Nov 2026), one Mac with Xcode, free Apple ID only, CI (GitHub-hosted) already producing an unsigned device IPA (`com.therapylocal.app`), goal "tap a link in Safari → app installs."

## Key Question 1: Free Apple ID ("personal team") provisioning in 2026 — exact limits and how cert + profile are created

### Takeaway
Apple officially documents the free "Personal Team" limits today as: 10 App IDs (expire after 7 days), 3 registered devices (expire after 7 days), 3 installed apps per device, and provisioning profiles that expire 7 days from issuance — all managed *only through Xcode* (no web portal, no App Store Connect API). The development certificate itself is a normal ~1-year dev cert that Xcode creates; third-party tools (Sideloadly, AltStore, SideStore) can create cert/profiles too by replaying Xcode's Apple-ID-backed provisioning API with "anisette" device-attestation data, and the resulting cert (p12) + profile (.mobileprovision) can technically be exported and used in CI.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's current official limits (fetched 2026-11-16): for a non-member account, "Xcode will indicate it's a Personal Team… Your account's App IDs, devices, certificates, and provisioning profiles are managed directly in Xcode, and you'll be required to reprovision your apps to a device periodically. You can register up to 10 App IDs, which expire after 7 days. You can register up to 3 devices, which expire after 7 days. You can install up to 3 apps per device. Provisioning profiles that enable apps to be installed on a device will expire 7 days from issuance. You'll need to rebuild and reinstall your app to your device after expiration." — [Apple Developer Help: Developer account overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/basics/about-your-developer-account)
- The same 10 App IDs / 3 devices / 3 apps / 7-day profile figures are independently confirmed by multiple 2026 articles — [BetaDrop, published 2026-11-09](https://betadrop.app/free-apple-developer-account); [Borel, 2026-09-26](https://borel.one/blog/iphone-app-expires-after-7-days); [RottenWiFi, 2026-08-07](https://blog.rottenwifi.com/how-to-create-a-free-apple-developer-account-and-link-it-to-xcode); [OpenV2 Docs](https://openv2.dev/docs/building/apple-account-limits)
- Free accounts have **no** access to Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles on the web and no App Store Connect: "The free tier has no access to Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles, so there is no way to register devices or create an Ad Hoc profile." — [Bimal Khatri, 2026-09-17](https://bimalkhatri.com.np/blogs/ad-hoc-distribution-ios); confirmed by Apple's page (personal-team assets "managed directly in Xcode") — [Apple Developer Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/basics/about-your-developer-account)
- A free Apple ID in Xcode gets a personal team "that you can use to launch apps on devices" — Xcode GUI is the sanctioned creation path for the dev cert and profile — [Apple: Add your Apple ID account (Xcode help)](https://help.apple.com/xcode/mac/current/en.lproj/devaf282080a.html)
- Device registration on a free team happens by pairing the iPhone to the Mac and running from Xcode (UDID registered automatically); after first pairing Xcode can install over Wi-Fi — [BetaDrop, 2026-11-09](https://betadrop.app/free-apple-developer-account). The free profile contains the registered devices' UDIDs (≤3), which is what makes the IPA device-locked.
- Non-Xcode creation is possible and is exactly what Sideloadly/AltStore/SideStore do: they "log into Apple with your Apple Account (using anisette data to satisfy Apple's device-attestation check), register App IDs, obtain an iOS development certificate, fetch provisioning profiles…, sign the IPA locally, and install it" — [kylosonic/ProxyTunnel research report on Sideloadly internals](https://github.com/kylosonic/ProxyTunnel/blob/master/docs/research/sideloadly-free-apple-id-report.md); Sideloadly's own FAQ confirms it hits Apple's free-account quota ("Your maximum App ID limit has been reached. You may create up to 10 App IDs every 7 days… This is a limitation set by Apple on free developer accounts") — [Sideloadly FAQ](https://sideloadly.io/faq)
- Exporting for CI is standard practice for paid teams and works identically for a personal-team dev cert: export the signing certificate from Keychain Access/Xcode as `.p12`, copy the `.mobileprovision` out of the provisioning-profiles folder, base64 them into GitHub secrets, import into a temporary keychain on the runner, and use manual signing — [GitHub Docs: Installing an Apple certificate on macOS runners](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/deploy/deploy-to-third-party-platforms/sign-xcode-applications); [Sasikanth, 2026-07-06](https://www.sasikanth.dev/managing-ios-signing-certificates-and-provisioning-profiles-in-github-actions); [Qiita guide, 2026-04-28 (Japanese)](https://qiita.com/haru-qiita/items/28598cfd94de977dd27c)
- Automated profile *download* from Apple requires an App Store Connect API key (`apple-actions/download-provisioning-profiles` takes `issuer-id` / `api-key-id` / `api-private-key`), which only exists for paid memberships — [Apple-Actions/download-provisioning-profiles](https://github.com/apple-actions/download-provisioning-profiles); TestFlight and App Store Connect sit behind the same $99/year — [BetaDrop, 2026-11-09](https://betadrop.app/free-apple-developer-account)
- Free-tier capability limits that could matter for the app itself: no push notifications, no in-app purchases, no network extension; App Groups technically listed as supported on the free tier but flaky in practice — [kylosonic report](https://github.com/kylosonic/ProxyTunnel/blob/master/docs/research/sideloadly-free-apple-id-report.md); [Borel, 2026-09-26](https://borel.one/blog/iphone-app-expires-after-7-days) (push, iCloud, Sign in with Apple, IAP unavailable)
- Each app *extension* consumes its own App ID from the 10-ID budget — [Scarlet iOS guide, updated 2026-11-01](https://scarletios.com/fix-free-apple-id-3-app-limit-iphone) (citing AltStore/SideStore docs)
- iOS-level change relevant to bundle IDs: "With the latest iOS versions, Apple has prevented users on free Apple accounts from sideloading apps that have the same bundle ID as an App Store app," forcing tools to mangle the bundle ID — [Sideloadly FAQ](https://sideloadly.io/faq)

### Inferences
- The certificate is NOT what expires after 7 days — the profile is. A free-team development certificate is a normal dev cert that outlives the weekly profile (explicitly stated by [BetaDrop](https://betadrop.app/free-apple-developer-account): "The certificate is not what expires — the provisioning profile is"). For CI this means: store the p12 once, and the recurring work is only getting a fresh 7-day profile.
- Getting a fresh profile is the weak link for full automation: free teams have no ASC API key, so profile creation needs either an Apple-ID session (Xcode manual step, or a scripted replay of the Apple-ID provisioning API with anisette — fragile, 2FA-dependent, and the approach used by all sideloading tools).
- A CI pipeline CAN sign weekly builds without any Apple ID interaction using the stored p12 — but only into a profile that still exists; since profiles die after 7 days, someone/something must recreate the profile at least weekly.
- `com.therapylocal.app` should be safe from the "same bundle ID as an App Store app" restriction unless that exact bundle ID is published on the App Store; worth a one-time check.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the exact validity period of a free-team development certificate (paid dev certs are 1 year; no source states the free-team cert lifetime explicitly — Apple's page only documents App IDs, devices and profiles).
- I found no explicit statement in Apple's developer agreement text (fetched only indirectly) that prohibits exporting a personal-team cert/p12 for signing in CI. The Apple Developer Program License Agreement text was not directly accessible in this session; the ToS-legal question is therefore unresolved. What IS documented: automated profile download requires an ASC API key (paid), and automating Apple ID sign-in is what triggers account-lock warnings in the sideloading ecosystem (see Q5, Anisette).

## Key Question 2: Does `itms-services://` OTA install still work in 2026 for a free-team development-signed IPA?

### Takeaway
Yes — the `itms-services` machinery is unchanged and still works in 2025–2026 (multiple 2026 guides + Apple's own device-management docs), and it works for *development-signed* IPAs as long as the device UDID is in the profile, with the first launch requiring an "Untrusted Developer" trust step and Developer Mode enabled. However, one 2026 source claims development profiles "won't work" for OTA — a conflict that appears to reflect ad-hoc-vs-development confusion, and the practical blockers for a free team are the 3-device/UDID ceiling, the 7-day profile expiry, and Developer Mode — not the OTA mechanism itself.

### Cited Findings
- The mechanism (unchanged, documented by Apple): a Safari-tapped link `itms-services://?action=download-manifest&url=<https manifest>` → iOS fetches the manifest over HTTPS → shows a confirmation dialog → downloads the IPA over HTTPS → validates signature + embedded profile against the device — [BetaDrop OTA guide, 2026-01-19](https://betadrop.app/blog/how-to-install-ipa-files-over-the-air-ota); [Decovar, 2025-04-03, with screenshot of the iOS "Install" dialog](https://decovar.dev/blog/2025/04/03/distributing-ios-applications-without-app-store); [Lars Richter, 2025-02-28](https://blog.lars-richter.dev/distribute-ios-apps-over-the-air)
- Exact manifest fields: `items → assets → software-package → url` (absolute HTTPS URL of the .ipa), `metadata` with `bundle-identifier` and `bundle-version` that must match the build, `title`, optional `display-image`/`full-size-image` — [BetaDrop OTA guide](https://betadrop.app/blog/how-to-install-ipa-files-over-the-air-ota); [Hashdrop guide](https://hashdrop.io/guides/install-ipa-over-the-air); [Decovar](https://decovar.dev/blog/2025/04/03/distributing-ios-applications-without-app-store)
- HTTPS is a hard requirement for both manifest and IPA ("needs to begin with https:" per Apple's Device Management documentation, checked 2026-09-03); self-signed or untrusted-chain certs fail before the install prompt — [BetaDrop OTA guide](https://betadrop.app/blog/how-to-install-ipa-files-over-the-air-ota); [Decovar](https://decovar.dev/blog/2025/04/03/distributing-ios-applications-without-app-store)
- Must be opened in Safari — in-app browsers and non-Safari browsers ignore the scheme ("the single most common report") — [Hashdrop](https://hashdrop.io/guides/install-ipa-over-the-air); [devhako, 2026-08-10](https://blog.devhako.com/journey/install-ios-app-over-the-air)
- Development-signed OTA works: "The profile must be Development or Ad-Hoc and must list every target device's UDID… For a development-signed build, the first launch shows 'Untrusted Developer' — trust it under Settings → General → VPN & Device Management" — [devhako, 2026-08-10](https://blog.devhako.com/journey/install-ios-app-over-the-air); also "a development-profile build installs over the air on your registered tester devices" — [Median.co, 2026-02-06](https://median.co/blog/how-to-install-ipa-on-ios-device-guide)
- **Direct contradiction:** "Your IPA must be signed with either an Ad Hoc or Enterprise provisioning profile. Development profiles won't work for OTA installation." — [DEV Community (Kaushal Rola), 2026-08-03](https://dev.to/kaushalrola/how-to-distribute-ios-apps-without-testflight-38ep). This conflicts with devhako and Median and with the free-team-specific analysis below; the mechanism has no documented gate that rejects development profiles (install acceptance is decided by the profile's device list, per BetaDrop: "no manifest can change that… the provisioning profile inside the build decides which devices accept it").
- Free-team-specific reality, stated precisely by BetaDrop (2026-11-09): it is a misconception that a free account can't export an IPA — "Xcode's Distribute App works for a Personal Team through its Debugging option, which produces a development-signed build you can absolutely put behind a link. The missing piece is narrower… a free account is never issued a distribution certificate, so it cannot produce the Release Testing build." The development-signed route then hits three limits: profile covers ≤3 devices (each registered via Xcode), expires in 7 days, and iOS 16+ requires Developer Mode before a development-signed build installs — [BetaDrop, 2026-11-09](https://betadrop.app/free-apple-developer-account)
- No new notarization requirement for OTA of dev-signed apps: Apple's notarization regime applies to EU *alternative distribution* (marketplaces/Web Distribution), not to the classic registered-devices OTA flow — [Apple: Changes for apps in the EU (fetched 2026-11-16)](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/). None of the 2025–2026 OTA guides mention any iOS 17/18/26 notarization gate for itms-services.
- One old-but-cited claim (2021): "Apps can't be distributed with a free Apple ID over the air… but they can be installed using iTunes Wi-Fi sync" — [XDA, 2021-11-06](https://www.xda-developers.com/how-to-sideload-apps-iphone-altstore). This describes AltStore's design decision rather than a platform block, and is contradicted by the 2026 sources above; treat as outdated/conflicting.
- Common failure modes documented in 2026: "Unable to Install" = device not in profile (or App Store profile used); "nothing happens" = HTTPS/URL/manifest problems or non-Safari context; grey stuck icon = IPA URL unreachable — [BetaDrop distribute guide, updated 2026-09-28](https://betadrop.app/blog/how-to-distribute-ios-apps-without-testflight); [IPAFlow, 2026-08-14](https://www.getipaflow.com/ipa/ota-installation)

### Inferences
- For this scenario (one known device), OTA install of a free-team dev-signed IPA should work end-to-end: CI signs the device IPA with the stored p12 + a profile that includes the phone's UDID, hosts .ipa + manifest.plist over HTTPS (e.g., GitHub Pages or any static host), and the user taps the link in Safari. First-ever install needs: Developer Mode on (one-time) + one trust prompt (one-time, or after cert changes).
- The Xcode "Include manifest for over-the-air installation" export option / `manifest` dict in ExportOptions.plist can generate the manifest automatically — [Lars Richter](https://blog.lars-richter.dev/distribute-ios-apps-over-the-air); [devhako](https://blog.devhako.com/journey/install-ios-app-over-the-air)
- The 2026-08-03 dev.to claim is the only source found asserting development profiles can't do OTA; given three other 2026 sources describing development-profile OTA (including one that does it as a product), the weight of evidence favors "works," but this should be validated empirically on the target phone before the report commits to it.

### Gaps
- No Apple primary source explicitly documents OTA installation of *development*-signed (as opposed to ad-hoc/in-house) IPAs; Apple's registered-devices guides focus on ad-hoc. The finding rests on reputable secondary sources plus the documented absence of any such restriction in Apple's install-chain descriptions.
- Could not verify whether iOS 26/27 changed anything about the `itms-services` flow itself (e.g., dialog wording); no release-note evidence of change surfaced in searches.

## Key Question 3: What happens at the 7-day expiry, and what re-sign/refresh cadence can CI automate?

### Takeaway
At expiry the app stops launching (icon goes grey/launch fails, with no advance warning); the fix is a rebuild + reinstall with a fresh profile, and reinstalling the same bundle ID updates in place and preserves data. A CI pipeline can automate the weekly re-sign + OTA reinstall; the only piece needing an Apple-ID session is re-creating the 7-day profile itself, so the realistic cadence is a weekly scheduled build/install, with on-device tools (SideStore) able to remove the weekly computer step entirely.

### Cited Findings
- Expiry behavior: "On the eighth day the app stops launching. The icon stays on the home screen, tapping it fails, and nothing tells you it is about to happen" — [BetaDrop, 2026-11-09](https://betadrop.app/free-apple-developer-account); "one day the icon is there, you tap it, and nothing happens, or iOS says the app is no longer available" — [Borel, 2026-09-26](https://borel.one/blog/iphone-app-expires-after-7-days)
- Apple's own remedy: "You'll need to rebuild and reinstall your app to your device after expiration" — [Apple Developer Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/basics/about-your-developer-account)
- Reinstall over the same app preserves data and doesn't consume a new App ID: "Reinstalling the same project normally updates the existing app" and "removing an installed app does not immediately free a registered App ID" — [OpenV2 Docs](https://openv2.dev/docs/building/apple-account-limits); "Usually not [data loss], if you reinstall the same app with the same identifier" — [Borel FAQ](https://borel.one/blog/iphone-app-expires-after-7-days); Sideloadly documents overwrite-install with same bundle ID + same Apple ID as the supported update path — [Sideloadly FAQ](https://sideloadly.io/faq)
- OTA update of an already-installed app is the same itms-services mechanism (iOS treats it as an update when bundle ID matches) — per the install-chain description in [BetaDrop distribute guide](https://betadrop.app/blog/how-to-distribute-ios-apps-without-testflight) (signature/profile revalidation on install)
- AltStore/SideStore automate refresh on-device: SideStore "will periodically 'refresh' your apps in the background, to keep their normal 7-day development period from expiring" — [SideStore docs FAQ](https://docs.sidestore.io/docs/faq); AltStore refreshes via AltServer on a LAN computer — [AltServer docs](https://faq.altstore.io/altstore-classic/altserver); Sideloadly has a desktop auto-refresh daemon that re-signs apps near expiry when the device is reachable via USB/Wi-Fi — [Sideloadly](https://sideloadly.io/) / [Sideloadly FAQ](https://sideloadly.io/faq)
- Background refresh has constraints: it needs the device unlocked ("You still need to use the device as it can only do the action when you [it]'s unlocked" — SideStore issue) — [SideStore issue #496](https://github.com/SideStore/SideStore/issues/496); iOS Shortcuts can schedule "Refresh All Apps" automations as a mitigation (same issue thread)
- CI-side signing without Apple ID interaction is fully documented (p12 + profile as secrets, temporary keychain, manual signing) — [GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/deploy/deploy-to-third-party-platforms/sign-xcode-applications); [Sasikanth, 2026-07-06](https://www.sasikanth.dev/managing-ios-signing-certificates-and-provisioning-profiles-in-github-actions)

### Inferences
- Least-painful CI cadence: a `schedule:`-triggered weekly workflow that (a) re-signs/rebuilds the IPA with the stored p12 and a fresh profile, (b) uploads .ipa + regenerated manifest.plist to a static HTTPS host, and (c) emails/links the itms-services URL. Tapping the link weekly = one tap, no computer. The profile-creation step is the part an Apple-ID session (Xcode) must supply; how often depends on whether the profile in the repo is still <7 days old.
- Fully computer-free refresh is achievable with SideStore (on-device re-sign every ~7 days, needs Wi-Fi + the device unlocked), removing the weekly CI install link entirely — see Q5.
- A 7-day timer on the *profile* also means the profile embedded in the hosted IPA expires even if the user never reinstalls; the hosted artifact must be replaced weekly (re-signed), not just re-linked.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how long before expiry a background refresh succeeds (e.g., SideStore's refresh schedule granularity); SideStore's own scheduling exists (6h/daily/weekly in a community build) — [SideStore discussion #1482, Sept 2026](https://github.com/orgs/SideStore/discussions/1482).
- Could not verify whether iOS warns at all before expiry on current versions (multiple sources say no warning).

## Key Question 4: EU specifics (Czech Republic) — AltStore PAL status, costs, arbitrary-IPA install, notarization

### Takeaway
AltStore PAL is alive and free to users in the EU (incl. Czech Republic) in 2026, but it is irrelevant for a free-team app: it only installs *notarized* apps, requires a paid Apple Developer account ($99/yr) and App Store Connect submission, and takes 0% commission while Apple's 5% Core Technology Commission applies to digital sales (CTF abolished Oct 1, 2026). The EU route therefore does NOT change the recommendation — AltStore Classic/SideStore/free provisioning apply worldwide, and Apple's official EU alternative-distribution channels (marketplaces, Web Distribution) all require the paid membership + notarization.

### Cited Findings
- AltStore's own comparison (current): **AltStore PAL** — "Available in the EU, Japan, & Brazil; Apps require Notarization; Paid Apple Developer account required ($99/year); 5% Apple Core Technology Commission; All app entitlements; No JIT; Apps never expire; Users can install unlimited apps." **AltStore Classic** — "Available worldwide; No Notarization required; No paid Apple Developer account required; No commission; Limited entitlements; JIT available; Users must refresh apps every 7 days; Users can install up to 3 apps at a time" — [developer.altstore.io](https://developer.altstore.io/)
- AltStore PAL requires submitting through App Store Connect with a paid account and Apple Notarization before distribution — [AltStore FAQ: Distribute with AltStore PAL](https://faq.altstore.io/developers/distribute-with-altstore-pal)
- PAL cannot install arbitrary IPAs: "AltStore PAL is a separate alternative-marketplace app and does not provide Classic's general IPA-import workflow" — [CloudsPress, 2026-08-08](https://www.cloudspress.com/how-to-sideload-an-ipa-on-iphone-with-altstore); "It installs only notarized apps… not any IPA file" — [Scarlet iOS, updated 2026-11-02](https://scarletios.com/altstore-pal-guide/)
- Fee state as of 2026: Apple's CTF (€0.50/first-annual-install) replaced by the **Core Technology Commission: 5% commission on digital goods and services** for apps distributed outside the App Store; free apps owe Apple nothing; AltStore takes 0% — [AltStore blog, 2026-10-19](https://blog.altstore.io/2026/10/19/big-changes-to-alternative-distribution.html); [Apple: Changes for apps in the EU (fetched 2026-11-16)](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/); [AltStore fees page](https://faq.altstore.io/developers/fees)
- Apple's official EU page (fetched 2026-11-16): License Agreement updated 2026-08-18; effective **2026-10-01**: unified business terms, CTF replaced by 5% CTC, Initial Acquisition Fee and Store Services Fee eliminated, and eligibility for operating marketplaces/Web Distribution expanded ("no longer required to have a legal entity or be established in the EU") — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/)
- **Web Distribution** (EU, iOS 17.5+/iPadOS 18+): distributes *notarized* apps from an Apple-authorized developer's own registered website; requires Apple Developer Program membership, Apple authorization, and eligibility criteria (financial-stability bar / public company / VC funding / audited / $1M letter of credit / 1M first annual installs) — [Apple: Web Distribution in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/support/web-distribution-eu/); [Apple: Changes for apps in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/)
- Notarization is a baseline for ALL EU alternative distribution ("applies to all apps, regardless of their distribution channel") — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/); user-side, EU users can install marketplaces and web-distributed apps, with a 90-day traveling grace period and new install UX coming in iOS/iPadOS 27.2 — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/); [Apple Support 118110, updated 2026-10-26](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118110)
- EU marketplaces in 2026: AltStore PAL (free to users since Epic's grant; was €1.50/yr), Epic Games Store, Aptoide, Onside (also Japan since Feb 2026), Skich; Setapp Mobile shut down 2026-02-16 — [Wolfpack Digital guide, updated 2026](https://www.wolfpack-digital.com/blogposts/exploring-third-party-stores-and-app-store-alternatives-a-guide-for-app-developers); [AppWinners, 2026-10-19](https://appwinners.eu/guides/alternative-app-stores-and-sideloading)
- Japan opened alternative distribution with iOS 26.2 and Brazil with iOS 26.5 — [Foresight Mobile, 2026-03-16 (updated)](https://foresightmobile.com/blog/ios-app-distribution-guide-2026)
- AltStore PAL latest version 2.3.2 (2026-06-15) — [Scarlet iOS, updated 2026-11-02](https://scarletios.com/altstore-pal-guide/)

### Inferences
- For a free Apple ID in the Czech Republic, every Apple-official EU channel (PAL, other marketplaces, Web Distribution) is blocked by the paid-membership + notarization requirements; the DMA route becomes viable only if the project ever buys the $99 membership (then AltStore PAL would remove the 7-day expiry and 3-app limit, with no Apple fee for a free app).
- AltStore Classic *is* usable in the Czech Republic (worldwide) but needs AltServer on the Mac for every install/refresh — strictly worse than SideStore for the "no computer" goal.
- The Oct 1, 2026 terms change (CTF→CTC) does not affect a free app distributed via free provisioning at all (no Apple fees, no agreement to sign beyond normal Apple ID usage).

### Gaps
- AltStore PAL's active user count is unpublished ("thousands" per a MacStories quote relayed by the ecosystem press) — [AppWinners](https://appwinners.eu/guides/alternative-app-stores-and-sideloading).
- Could not verify whether AltStore PAL onboarding for new developers has any waitlist/manual step in Nov 2026 (its "Get Started" implies immediate availability).

## Key Question 5: On-device signing tools in 2026 — SideStore, Sideloadly, eSign/Scarlet and friends

### Takeaway
SideStore is the one tool that satisfies "download IPA on the phone → install, no computer": alive and actively maintained (0.7.0-alpha, Sept 2026), needs a computer exactly once (the 2026 method uses `iloader`, with the App Store app **LocalDevVPN** replacing the old WireGuard/StosVPN trick), then installs and refreshes IPAs on-device over Wi-Fi. Sideloadly and AltStore Classic are alive but need a computer reachable for every install/refresh. eSign's original is discontinued (2025) with forks/rebuilds circulating; Scarlet rides a shared enterprise certificate with revocation risk; TrollStore is dead for current iOS (14.0–16.6.1/17.0 only).

### Cited Findings
- **SideStore**: "An alternative app store for seamless sideloading. No jailbreak needed, and **no computer** after the initial install, only a Wi-Fi connection" — [sidestore.io](https://sidestore.io/); "resigns apps with your personal development certificate, and then uses a specially designed VPN in order to trick iOS into installing them… You only need a computer once during installation" — [SideStore docs FAQ](https://docs.sidestore.io/docs/faq); open source AGPL-3.0, fork of AltStore — [GitHub: SideStore/SideStore](https://github.com/SideStore/SideStore)
- 2026 setup path: first install via a desktop tool (`iloader`), then the App Store app **LocalDevVPN** (publisher Coxson Engineering LLC) is connected whenever SideStore installs/updates/refreshes; Developer Mode required on iOS 16+; supports iOS/iPadOS 15.0–17.7.7 and 18.0+; latest release 0.7.0-alpha (2026-09-15) — [SideStore docs: Install](https://docs.sidestore.io/docs/installation/install); [Scarlet iOS SideStore guide, updated 2026-11-02](https://scarletios.com/how-to-install-sidestore-on-iphone/)
- The no-computer trick mechanics: SideStore simulates a computer with iTunes over a virtual network — historically a WireGuard tunnel to a server on the device itself (works on Wi-Fi only; iOS allows only one VPN at a time), and the SideStore team's dedicated **StosVPN**; a 2026 technical writeup details both, noting StosVPN's background-disconnect problems — [Lan Tian's blog, 2026-05-01](https://lantian.pub/en/article/modify-computer/sidestore-without-stosvpn-across-lan.lantian/). Community work in Sept 2026 achieved PC-free scheduled refresh using the official LocalDevVPN + Apple's CoreDevice stack, verified on iOS 26.6.1 — [SideStore discussion #1482, 2026-09-06](https://github.com/orgs/SideStore/discussions/1482)
- Installing an IPA downloaded in Safari with no computer: after setup, SideStore can install IPAs on-device (import via the + button / from Files) and add custom sources; it also supports OTA self-updates and can adopt/re-sign apps previously installed by Sideloadly/AltStore without data loss — [SideStore docs FAQ](https://docs.sidestore.io/docs/faq); [Ichitaso walkthrough (SideStore "+ button" installs new IPA files)](https://ichitaso.com/iphone/how-to-use-sidestore); [Scarlet iOS SideStore guide](https://scarletios.com/how-to-install-sidestore-on-iphone/) ("From now on you can install IPA files and add sources inside SideStore… as long as you are on Wi-Fi and LocalDevVPN is connected")
- Caveats: a pairing file is needed and can expire after iOS updates/resets (fix requires the computer); shared/old Anisette servers have caused Apple ID locks — SideStore recommends official or self-hosted anisette and a burner Apple ID — [SideStore docs: Install](https://docs.sidestore.io/docs/installation/install); [SideStore docs FAQ](https://docs.sidestore.io/docs/faq)
- **Sideloadly**: alive, v0.71.0, Windows/macOS, free, funded by Patreon; works with free or paid Apple IDs; Wi-Fi sideloading; auto-refresh daemon re-signs before expiry while the computer is reachable; free-ID limits are Apple's (7 days, 3 apps, 10 App IDs) — [sideloadly.io](https://sideloadly.io/); [Sideloadly FAQ](https://sideloadly.io/faq); internals (anisette, cert/profile creation via Apple's API, libimobiledevice install) — [kylosonic report](https://github.com/kylosonic/ProxyTunnel/blob/master/docs/research/sideloadly-free-apple-id-report.md)
- **AltStore Classic**: worldwide, no paid account, but "AltServer must be running on a computer connected to the same WiFi as AltStore in order to sideload or refresh apps" (or USB) — [AltStore FAQ: AltServer](https://faq.altstore.io/altstore-classic/altserver); it *can* import an IPA from the iPhone's Files app, but signing/installing still goes through AltServer — [CloudsPress, 2026-08-08](https://www.cloudspress.com/how-to-sideload-an-ipa-on-iphone-with-altstore)
- **eSign**: on-device signer (takes .p12 + .mobileprovision, re-signs any IPA, installs with no computer); "the original was discontinued in 2025 and its servers went dark; what circulates under that name today is a mix of community rebuilds and sites selling certificates" — [builds.io, 2026-09-02](https://builds.io/blog/technologies/ios-technologies/best-ipa-signer-iphone); community forks/rebuilds (eSign+, Feather) — [LibShortcut, 2026-05-30](https://libshortcut.com/blog/install-ipa-with-esign); certificate sources for eSign are typically shared/enterprise certs with revocation risk — [builds.io eSign on iOS 26, 2026-08-25](https://builds.io/blog/technologies/ios-technologies/esign-ios-26-ipa-signing/)
- **Scarlet** and similar "stores": install IPAs worldwide using a shared enterprise certificate — no setup, but "Apple revokes these certificates when it notices public use, and then every app signed with it stops opening" — [Scarlet iOS tools comparison, 2026-03-09](https://scarletios.com/free-sideloading-tools-compared-2026-edition) (note: scarletios.com is Scarlet's own promotional site — use with caution); third-party sellers of "developer certificates" share certs that can be revoked — [Scarlet iOS 3-app guide](https://scarletios.com/fix-free-apple-id-3-app-limit-iphone)
- **TrollStore** (cert-free via CoreTrust bug) only supports iOS 14.0–16.6.1 and iOS 17.0 — useless on a current (iOS 26.x) phone — [Scarlet iOS tools comparison](https://scarletios.com/free-sideloading-tools-compared-2026-edition)
- **Feather / KSign**: cited as the maintained "bring your own certificate" on-device signers ("otherwise Ksign is the maintained version of the same thing" — [builds.io, 2026-09-02](https://builds.io/blog/technologies/ios-technologies/best-ipa-signer-iphone))
- Ecosystem context for tool risk: apps signed with your *own* Apple ID are not revoked for public use, unlike shared certs — [Scarlet iOS comparison](https://scarletios.com/free-sideloading-tools-compared-2026-edition)

### Inferences
- For the "tap a link in Safari → installs, no computer per install" goal there are two realistic designs: (a) CI OTA + weekly re-sign (pure Apple machinery, zero third-party apps, but the 7-day profile must be refreshed via an Apple-ID session somewhere), or (b) one-time SideStore setup, then CI publishes the IPA and SideStore installs + auto-refreshes it forever without any computer (needs the app in a SideStore/AltStore-format "source" or manual import from Files).
- SideStore route caveat: it consumes 1 of the 3 free-app slots (SideStore itself counts), uses the same free Apple ID, and needs Wi-Fi + unlocked device for refreshes; the pairing-file expiry after iOS updates is the main recurring maintenance event.
- eSign/KSign/Feather with a *free personal* cert would also achieve on-device install of a downloaded IPA, but they are third-party of varying provenance; with SideStore as a maintained AGPL option, the gray-market signers add risk without adding capability.

### Gaps
- Could not verify SideStore's current support status for iOS 26.x from primary docs (its install page lists up to "iOS/iPadOS 18.0+" and "iOS/iPadOS 15.0-15.8.6 / 16.0-17.7.7 / 18.0+"; community discussion #1482 reports verification on iOS 26.6.1 in Sept 2026).
- Scarlet's official site and exact current cert status could not be verified from a neutral source (only its own promotional site, which itself admits revocation risk).
- No independent maintenance-status verification for KSign/Feather beyond the builds.io article.

## Key Question 6: Apple policy changes 2024–2026 that alter this picture

### Takeaway
The DMA opened EU-only alternative distribution (marketplaces iOS 17.4, Web Distribution iOS 17.5) — both paywalled behind the $99 membership + notarization, so they never threatened the free-team OTA route. The fee landscape flipped on 2026-10-01 (CTF → 5% CTC, EU Addendum folded into the standard License Agreement), notarization remains mandatory for EU alternative channels but was never extended to dev-signed device installs, and no 2024–2026 change closed the classic free-provisioning/itms-services path — though iOS-level rules (Developer Mode since iOS 16; free accounts blocked from sideloading bundle IDs that exist on the App Store) tightened the edges.

### Cited Findings
- 2024: iOS 17.4 brought EU alternative app marketplaces; Web Distribution announced March 2024, enabled in iOS 17.5 (betas Apr 2024) — [Apple news via Gigazine, 2024-03-13](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240313-apple-app-web-distribution); [Thurrott, 2024-04-16](https://www.thurrott.com/mobile/300914/ios-17-5-beta-2-enables-downloading-iphone-apps-directly-from-websites-in-the-eu); Web Distribution devices min. iOS 17.5/iPadOS 18 — [Apple: Web Distribution in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/support/web-distribution-eu/)

### Inferences
- Nothing in 2024–2026 policy changes the recommendation for a free Apple ID: the classic personal-team + itms-services OTA path remains untouched (it predates the DMA and is not part of Apple's "alternative distribution" regime at all), while every DMA-era channel requires the paid membership.
- If the project ever pays $99/yr, the picture changes materially: TestFlight (90-day builds, 10k testers), ad-hoc OTA (year-long profiles, 100 devices), AltStore PAL (no expiry, no notarization-free gaps) all unlock — relevant as the "exit ramp" for this pipeline.
- The 5% CTC is only a risk for this app if it ever sells digital goods through an EU alternative channel; a free app distributed via free provisioning owes Apple nothing.
