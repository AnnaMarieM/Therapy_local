# AD-07: The diarizer finds the number of speakers itself; it is never forced to two

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted |
| Decided | `2026-11-08 23:52 CET` |
| Decided by | Martin Hajný (@hajnina) |
| Drafted by | Claude (Opus), PR 1 transcript pipeline |

## Context
- [AD-06](AD-06.md) (proposed in the same PR) adds speaker diarization: sherpa-onnx `OfflineSpeakerDiarization` runs over the whole recording in `:batch`, then each speaker turn is transcribed.
- Its clustering takes either a fixed speaker count (`numClusters`) or, with `numClusters = -1`, a distance threshold (`DiarizeRunner.CLUSTER_THRESHOLD = 0.5`, the value sherpa-onnx's examples use) that decides the count from the audio.
- The pipeline passes the count as a hint, `DIARIZE_SPEAKERS_HINT`; a value above 0 becomes `numClusters`, 0 means the threshold decides.
- Most sessions are one therapist and one patient, but not all: couples and family sessions have three or more voices, and a recording can hold only one voice (the patient did not come through, a dictated note, a phone on the far side of the room).
- The TRANSCRIBE fallback in AD-06 takes the chunk path when fewer than two speakers are found, and the role guess maps every speaker other than the therapist and the patient to "other".

## Decision
1. **`DIARIZE_SPEAKERS_HINT = 0`: the clustering threshold decides how many speakers there are.** The pipeline never forces a count.
2. One voice found ⇒ the AD-06 fallback (chunk path, speaker unknown, `diarization_skipped reason=one_speaker`), and no role guess.
3. Three or more voices found ⇒ the transcript keeps them all (`S0`, `S1`, `S2`, …); the role guess names one therapist and one patient, the others are "other" and are labelled by number in the transcript.
4. Forcing a count (for example a per-session "two people" setting) is a later decision, made from measurements, not a default.

## Alternatives considered
- **Force two speakers (`DIARIZE_SPEAKERS_HINT = 2`).** More robust for the common one-to-one session, since the threshold cannot split one voice in two or merge two similar voices. Rejected: it silently mislabels couples and family sessions (a third voice is assigned to the therapist or the patient), and it hides a recording with only one voice, which then gets an invented second speaker and a meaningless role guess.
- **A per-client or per-session speaker-count setting.** Rejected for now: it is UI (owned by someone else) and a new setting, and there is no measurement yet showing the threshold is wrong often enough to need it.

## Consequences
- **Better:**
  - group sessions and one-voice recordings are reported as they are, not bent into two speakers;
  - the one-voice case is visible in the logs (`reason=one_speaker`).
- **Worse, accepted:**
  - the threshold can split one voice into two clusters (a voice that changes with emotion, distance from the phone, room noise) or merge two similar voices; one-to-one sessions can then show a spurious third speaker labelled "other";
  - the "Is this incorrect?" swap in PR 1 only exchanges therapist and patient; a wrongly split or extra speaker cannot be reassigned yet.
- **Still true:** the count is one constant; changing it needs a new decision that supersedes this one.

## Verification
- **Unit tests:** the hint 0 reaches sherpa-onnx as `numClusters = -1` with the threshold; a hint above 0 becomes `numClusters`; one speaker found takes the fallback; three speakers give `S0`..`S2` and a map with one "other".
- **On-device:** on two-person recordings (Czech and English), log the speaker count from `diarize_done speakers=<k>`. If a spurious extra speaker is common, revisit the threshold or the forced count in a new decision.
