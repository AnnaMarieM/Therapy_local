# AD-06: Speaker diarization is a model role; diarize first, then transcribe per turn

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted |
| Decided | `2026-11-08 23:54 CET` |
| Decided by | Martin Hajný (@hajnina) |
| Drafted by | Claude (Sonnet subagent), PR 1 transcript pipeline |

## Context
- Recordings are cut into 20 s chunks with a 5 s overlap and each chunk goes through Canary (`CanaryRunner`, `EngineOp.Transcribe`). `SegmentBuilder` stitches the overlaps. Nothing in the app knows who is speaking, and the transcript has no speaker.
- The product needs two things from the speakers: a transcript labelled by speaker, and a guess which one is the therapist and which one the patient (later PRs).
- Canary returns text only, with no word timestamps. A chunk that contains a change of speaker cannot be split by speaker without guessing where the change falls in the text.
- sherpa-onnx 2.0.0, already in the app (`app/libs/sherpa-onnx-2.0.0.aar`), ships `OfflineSpeakerDiarization`: pyannote segmentation, a speaker-embedding extractor and clustering, in one call over a whole recording.
- AD-02 says one model per role, and a new model needs a decision. AD-01 says inference runs in `:live` and `:batch`. The pipeline's TRANSCRIBE job already runs in `:batch`.
- A 60-minute session is 57.6 M samples at 16 kHz: about 115 MB as PCM16 and 230 MB as float.

## Decision
1. **Speaker diarization is a new model role** with one model (AD-02, point 7): sherpa-onnx `OfflineSpeakerDiarization` with
   - segmentation: pyannote segmentation-3.0 (`model.onnx`, about 6 MB, MIT), converted by k2-fsa;
   - speaker embedding: WeSpeaker ResNet34 trained on VoxCeleb (`wespeaker_en_voxceleb_resnet34_LM.onnx`, about 26.5 MB, Apache-2.0), the embedding that pyannote's own 3.1 pipeline pairs with segmentation-3.0. VoxCeleb has speakers of many nationalities and languages, and the model compares voices, not words, so Czech is not a special case. The 3D-Speaker models in the same release are trained on Chinese data.
   - Both files are one `ModelSpec` (`diarization-pyannote3-wespeaker`), part of the required set like Canary, downloaded by the same worker, with sizes and SHA-256 pinned.
2. **It runs in `:batch`** through `EngineOp.Diarize` (a new `EngineKind.DIARIZE`) on the CPU through the same backend ladder as Canary. The PCM16 of the whole recording crosses the process boundary through shared memory, is converted to float inside the host, and only the turns come back (start ms, end ms, speaker number).
3. **Diarize first, then transcribe per turn.** TRANSCRIBE diarizes the whole recording, cuts the audio at the turn boundaries, transcribes each piece with Canary, and joins neighbouring pieces of one speaker into an utterance. The speaker count is a hint when known (`numClusters`), else the clustering threshold decides.
4. **Fallback to the chunk path.** If the model is missing, the diarizer fails, or it finds one speaker, TRANSCRIBE runs exactly as today and every utterance has an unknown speaker. The reason is logged (`diarization_skipped reason=...`). Diarization never fails the job.
5. **One model resident at a time.** `ModelPool.diarizer()` unloads the LLM, the embedder and Canary before it loads, and they unload it. A transcribe job therefore gets the diarizer and Canary one after the other.

## Alternatives considered
- **Cluster EmbeddingGemma audio embeddings.** The embedder already maps audio into a 768-d space. Rejected: it is trained for retrieval of meaning, not for telling voices apart; the lookback windows it embeds are seconds long and cut across turns; and it lives in `:live`, so batch work would need the live process.
- **Keep the 20 s chunks and guess which words belong to which speaker by spreading them evenly.** Rejected: Canary has no word timestamps, so every change of speaker inside a chunk would be a guess, and the turn-taking in therapy (short replies) makes that the common case, not the exception.
- **Diarize each chunk on its own.** Rejected: speaker numbers would not be stable between chunks, so S0 in chunk 3 could be S1 in chunk 4. Clustering over the whole recording is what keeps them consistent.
- **A cloud diarization service.** Rejected: audio and transcripts stay on the phone.

## Consequences
- **Better:**
  - transcripts and later facts can carry a speaker;
  - turn boundaries are real, so a transcribed piece has one speaker and no overlap stitching inside a turn;
  - the sherpa-onnx runtime is already shipped, so no new native library.
- **Worse, accepted:**
  - about 32.5 MB more to download for every install;
  - memory in `:batch` while diarizing a session: about 115 MB for the PCM16 plus 230 MB for the float copy at 60 minutes (about 345 MB, before the models and the clustering). `MAX_SAMPLES` caps one call at two hours; a longer recording takes the chunk path;
  - TRANSCRIBE takes longer by the diarization time, and short turns are transcribed with less context than a 20 s chunk, which may cost accuracy on one-word answers;
  - diarization quality on Czech therapy audio (one phone, two people, room noise) is not measured yet.
- **Still true:** Canary is the only speech recogniser; the LLM never gets audio; the `:batch` host contract stays one op per native call.

## Verification
- **Unit tests:** the model spec (two files, pinned sizes and SHA-256, unique ids); the turn wire format; `ModelPool` unloads the diarizer for the LLM, the embedder and Canary and the other way round.
- **Instrumented tests:** `EngineCodecTest` round-trips `Diarize` and its turns, below and above the inline limit.
- **On-device tests:** the self-test on open runs 2 s of silence and expects no turns; a two-speaker recording gives two speakers; a 60-minute recording completes in `:batch` without a kill, with the memory peak (PSS) logged and `diarize_done` showing `wall_ms` and `backend`.
- **Logging:** `diarize_done turns=<n> speakers=<k> audio_sec=<s> wall_ms=<ms> backend=<b>`; a failure is `diarize_failed` plus the engine's `engine_call_failed`. Counts and times only, never audio or text.
