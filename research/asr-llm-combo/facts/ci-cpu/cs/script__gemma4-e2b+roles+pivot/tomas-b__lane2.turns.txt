client: My wife. She says it can't go on like this anymore. And I don't either.
therapist: What can't go on?
client: Thirty times a night I check if I locked the door and turned off the stove, and it takes me an hour to leave the apartment.
therapist: An hour. What does it look like?
client: I lock the door, go to the elevator, come back, and pull the latch. Then I check the stove, every knob separately. Then the door again.
therapist: And when you do that, what happens?
client: I feel relieved for a moment. And then an image comes to my head that I didn't do it properly, and I have to do it again.
therapist: What is that image?
client: The apartment in flames. I see it clearly, like a movie.
therapist: How often does this happen to you?
client: Every day. It's worst in the evening when we go out or sleep. In the morning too.
therapist: How long has this been going on?
client: It started two years ago, when it was burning at the neighbors' place and we had to evacuate.
therapist: Tell me more about it.
client: It was at night. The alarm woke me up. There was smoke and people screaming in the hallway. We ran out in our pajamas.
therapist: How were you then?
client: That this is the end. No one was injured, but the whole thing burned down the neighbors' place. I stood outside in the winter and watched the windows burn.
therapist: And since then?
client: Since then I have the feeling that I can cause it. That if I don't control something, it will happen and I will be to blame.
therapist: That is a big responsibility.
client: I know that doesn't make sense. I know that logic. Still, it can't be turned off.
therapist: What do you do when you don't want to do it?
client: I try to leave it. And then anxiety grows in me, until I feel like I'm going crazy. I'd rather do it.
therapist: How does the anxiety manifest?
client: Heart, sweat. My hands are trembling and I feel sick to my stomach.
therapist: Who knows about it?
client: Barbora. My wife. At first she took it as a habit, now she sees that it's more.
therapist: How does she react?
client: She is patient, but I see that it is exhausting her. Yesterday we went to the cinema forty minutes later.
therapist: What happened?
client: She waited outside until I returned and checked the stove for the third time. She didn't say anything, just looked at me.
therapist: How were you?
client: Hanba. I felt like a child who can't get out of the house.

client: I work as an electrician in Olomouc. I do wiring in houses and sometimes in family homes.
therapist: How does that relate to checking?
client: A lot. I'm afraid that because of my head, I will make a mistake once. That I will connect a wire incorrectly and something will burn.
therapist: Has something like that happened to you?
client: No, never. But after every job I go back and check. Sometimes I'll go back the next day, just to look.

client: He thinks I'm a pedant. My boss tells me that I am the most meticulous in the company, but he doesn't see how much time that costs.
therapist: How much time does that cost?
