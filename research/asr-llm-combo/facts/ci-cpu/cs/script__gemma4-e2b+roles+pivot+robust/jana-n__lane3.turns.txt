therapist: Good day, Mrs. Nováková, please continue. What would you like to start with today?
client: Good day. I don't even know. I have a feeling someone pulled the rug out from under me.
therapist: What happened?
client: After fifteen years of marriage, my husband Radek and I decided on a divorce. And I still can't understand it.
therapist: Tell me more.
client: We were on vacation in Croatia this summer and everything seemed normal. And in September, he told me that it couldn't go on like this anymore.
therapist: How did you accept it?
client: I sat in the kitchen and couldn't say anything. Then I went to wash the dishes, as if nothing was happening.
therapist: What was happening inside you?
client: Emptiness. And then sadness. Then emptiness again. It alternates several times a day.
therapist: Did Radek explain to you why?
client: He says we gave up. That we live next to each other. I didn't notice, or I didn't want to notice.
therapist: What are you thinking about now?
client: That I should have seen it. That I am blind. I keep going back in my head and looking for where I made a mistake.
therapist: And did you find it?
client: No. Sometimes I think I was too tired and didn't care about it, other times I thought I cared too much.
therapist: It seems like you are really troubled by this.
client: Yes. And especially because of the children.
therapist: Tell me about them.
client: I have two children. Anička is twelve and Matěj is eight. And I don't know how to tell them.
therapist: You haven't told them yet?
client: No. Radek wants us to tell them together, but we keep putting it off. Anička is already hinting at something.
therapist: What does that tell you?
client: Last week she asked me if I argue with Dad. I told her no. I felt terrible.
therapist: Why terribly?
client: Because I lied. And she found out, I saw it in her eyes.
therapist: What was on your mind?
client: That I will take her away from life. That I will take her home.
therapist: How would you manage to tell her the truth?
client: I would rather have it behind me. But I am afraid of her tears. And Matěj is still little, he doesn't understand anything.
therapist: What would you want to tell him?
client: That we both like him and that it won't change. But I don't know if it's true, when I don't know how it will look.
therapist: What will it look like with the living situation?
client: The apartment is on a mortgage. Radek is probably moving to his brother, at least for now. The children will stay with me.
therapist: How do you cope with that?
client: I am afraid that I won't manage it alone. Radek always dealt with money.
therapist: What do you do on weekdays?
client: I work as a Czech teacher at a primary school in Brno. I teach ninth and eighth graders.
therapist: How do you study now?
client: I go to class with a dumpling in my throat. I feel like everyone sees that my life is falling apart.
therapist: Does anyone at school know about it?
client: Only the principal. I told her because I once cried on the hallway. She was kind.
therapist: What helped you?
client: That she didn't let me leave earlier. She gave me an hour off. But then I sat at home and it was worse.
therapist: Worse, why?
client: Because I was alone with my head. At work, there is at least something to do.
therapist: How do you sleep?
client: Poorly. Every night I wake up at three in the morning and think about what's next.
therapist: What do you say when you wake up?
client: That I don't have a mortgage, I don't have a husband, I have nothing. Then I calculate money, then I imagine how I will tell the children. It goes in circles.
therapist: And then?
client: I fall asleep around five, and I get up at half past seven. I am tired already in the morning.
therapist: Are you eating, moving?
client: I eat little. I have lost four kilos. My friend says I look terrible.
therapist: Do you have someone around you to talk to?
client: I have my sister Alena in Olomouc. She calls me almost every evening. And my friend Eva from work.
therapist: What do you tell them?
client: Alena almost everything. Eva only that I am going through a difficult time. I don't want her to think something about me.
therapist: What are you afraid she will think?
client: That I have failed. That a woman who cannot maintain a marriage is not a good example for children.
therapist: That is a difficult thought. Where does it come from?
client: Probably from my mother. She always said that marriage holds, whatever it wants.
therapist: What did she say when you told her?
client: She was silent. Then she said she hopes I thought it through well. She hasn't called much since then.
therapist: How are you feeling?
client: Sad. I would like her to hug me.
therapist: What would you like to tell her?
client: That it hurts. That I don't want to be alone. But it doesn't happen in our case.
therapist: What happens when you say it out loud?
client: I'm crying. I'm sorry.
therapist: There's nothing to apologize for. Take your time.
client: Thank you. I've never said it like that to anyone.
therapist: What is making you say that here?
client: It's strange to sit here and say all that out loud, but it helps a little.
therapist: What could help you the most right now?
client: Sleep. And know how to tell the children.
therapist: Let's take it step by step. Have you tried anything about sleep?
client: Chamomile tea, and that's all. The doctor offered me pills, but I don't want them.
therapist: Why don't you want them?
client: I'm afraid I won't be able to wake up in time for the children. And I don't want to be dependent.
therapist: That's reasonable. Try something small tonight, maybe leave a piece of paper by the bed and write down what wakes you up?
client: I could. At least I'll get it off my chest.
therapist: And about the children. What are you most afraid of?
client: That I will panic and cry. That I won't be able to stay calm.
therapist: What if it didn't bother you?
client: That I would cry in front of children? It would be scary for them.
therapist: Or they could see that sadness is allowed.
client: It didn't occur to me. Anička is sensitive, maybe that would help her.
therapist: We can look at how to prepare it next time. What do you take away from today?
client: That I am not alone and that I don't have to handle it right away.
therapist: Exactly. For today, we will end here.
client: Thank you, see you next week.
